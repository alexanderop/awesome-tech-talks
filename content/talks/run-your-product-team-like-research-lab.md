## Summary

Dan Shipper is co-founder and CEO of Every. He gave this talk at the Lenny and Friends Summit in San Francisco in September 2026, a week after new frontier models launched. His question: how do you keep building a product when what AI can do changes every few months?

His answer is that exploring the frontier and executing a roadmap pull in opposite directions. Exploring is divergent and throws most work away. Executing is convergent and says no to things. When everyone on the product team does both, the early adopters distract everyone else. So he separates the two into a labs team and a product team.

He then covers how to run the lab (tiny teams, dogfooding, parallel experiments) and how to move results into the product through a research pipeline. His example is "Kate bench", an agent trained on three years of his editor-in-chief's copy edits that moved from a side experiment to internal use.

## Key takeaways

- **Separate exploring from executing.** A labs team explores new model capabilities and expects to throw away about 90% of what it builds; the product team scales what works and adopts about 10% of it.
- **A labs team can be one person.** With current agents, a single person who tries each new model and reports back is enough to start.
- **Use two-slice teams, not two-pizza teams.** One or two people per experiment avoids coordination overhead; pair a "pirate" who prototypes fast and messy with an "architect" who turns a working mess into a real system.
- **Dogfood on real work.** The tightest feedback loop is building for yourself; use experiments for actual work to tell "useful" apart from "new".
- **Run competing experiments in parallel.** Several people attacking the same problem from different angles maps an unknown frontier faster than one coherent plan.
- **Make thrown-away work pay off.** Every turns failed experiments into published content, feeds an early-adopter program, and shares capability findings with the product team.
- **Promote ideas with explicit criteria.** Every reviews its pipeline weekly at all-hands and asks: do people use it and come back, is it 10x better than what exists, and can we afford to serve it?
- **Labs can replace the main product.** Anthropic Labs produced Claude Code, MCP and skills; OpenAI's Codex team started outside the main app and its desktop app later became the base of ChatGPT.
